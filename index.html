Before we talk

We already have strong engineers. Why bring you in?

Your team keeps ownership. I bring focused time to a recurring agent-workflow problem, implement the agreed changes, and document how they work. You can inspect my public engineering projects before we talk.

Do we need to share our code on the first call?

No. Describe one task, the tools involved, and where you had to intervene. We can discuss what access would be needed before agreeing to any paid work.

What happens after the discovery call?

If there’s a fit, I propose a written scope with deliverables, a fee, a timeline, and acceptance checks. You decide whether to proceed. The call creates no obligation to buy.

Is this only for Claude Code?

Claude Code is a particular focus. The engagement starts with your actual tools and constraints; verification, integrations, and handoffs often extend beyond one coding agent.

Do we have to adopt your projects?

No. I can improve your existing setup. I recommend one of my tools only when it fits the problem, and we agree on the choice before implementation.

What experience can we inspect?

At Zendesk, I led a zero-downtime etcd migration across production Kubernetes clusters and integrated diagnostics across 95% of production services. I also ran a development consultancy and founded Zatiti, which reached 50 paying retailers. My current agent tools are linked above; they demonstrate engineering work, not client consulting results.

Can you guarantee agents will ship autonomously?

No. We agree on a specific result we can test, including where a person still needs to review or approve work. That makes the engagement assessable without promising unattended software delivery.

What should I bring to the first call?

Your current agent setup, one task that went wrong or took too much supervision, and what a better outcome would look like. A public example or a description is enough to start.

Where does your agent workflow get stuck?

Tell me what you use, where you keep stepping in, and what you want to finish. We will use the first conversation to decide on a useful next step.
